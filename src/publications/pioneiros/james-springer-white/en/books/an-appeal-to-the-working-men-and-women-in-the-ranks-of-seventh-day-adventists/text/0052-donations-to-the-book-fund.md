It will be seen by the foregoing that the sum of $13,912.02 has been donated to the Book Fund in less than three years, while, during the same time, the sum of $6,782.52 has been paid out of this Fund for publications which have been gratuitously circulated, leaving in the treasury the sum of $7,129.50.

Abundant fruit of this work of liberality and love has already appeared in the conversion of many who have simply read our publications; but eternity alone will reveal the full amount of good accomplished.

The future is bright with promise of glorious success in this department of the work. The formation of Tract and Missionary Societies in all parts of the field, will give our publications a more judicious, extensive, and abundant circulation. As our people enter upon this work everywhere, it will be seen that the fund already in the treasury is just what is needed, and with cheerful hands they will put into it of their surplus means still more liberally.

The one-hundred-dollar men will want, during 1872, to increase from two to four hundred each. The fifty and twenty-five-dollar men should come up to one or two hundred, and many of the ten and five should rank among the fifty and one-hundred-dollar men. While all who represent less than five dollars are invited to come up to ten, or five at the very least.

It will be seen by the foregoing names of donors that, probably, not a fifth of our people are represented at all in this catalogue. All our new friends and brethren should have a cordial invitation to join us in this good work. Some who have borne the burden in the heat of the day had the blessed privilege of sacrificing all along the early period in the cause. We say, Let those who enter the cause at the eleventh hour, fresh for the work, have their penny. But we suggest that the Master would have them work in good earnest during the one hour. Come, friends, let us all come up to the help of the Lord, to the help of the Lord against the mighty. With usual prosperity, ours shall come up to four hundred on the present truth Book Fund before January 1873.

With an increasing demand for our publications, consequently a large increase in the work of publishing, our tracts can be furnished at the Office to Tract and Missionary Societies at one-half published prices. The fund now in the treasury enables the Association to purchase stock at lowest figures, and conduct the publishing work throughout on the most economical plan.

The raising of this fund has been one of the noblest acts of our people. It enables us to make the above statement, that with an increase of business, calling for editions of from fifteen to thirty thousand copies of tracts at one setting of the types, our tracts can be furnished at the Office at above prices. And then, if all our people put free-will offerings into the Book Fund, in a liberal manner, so that the treasury shall be bountifully replenished, one-half of the remaining half can be paid from the Book Fund, leaving our Tract and Missionary Societies to pay only one-fourth published prices. In this case Tract and Missionary Societies would receive thirty-two hundred pages of tracts for the small sum of one dollar. These statements are made from experience and safe calculation, and the friends of the cause may depend upon their correctness.
